# SP12.4 — Switch configuration: seams for Goal 9B

**Issue:** bedaHovorka/interlockSim#1170 (Goal 1B, SP12 leaf 4, parent #1159) · **Date:** 2026-10-11

SP12.4 moves the switch side of a route candidate out of `DefaultPathReservationService` into
`SwitchConfigurator` (same package, `internal`). It is a pure move: the code is verbatim, the
service keeps the orchestration and calls the new unit. The moved code logs under its own logger,
`cz.vutbr.fit.interlockSim.context.navigation.SwitchConfigurator` (the SP12 convention: one logger
per class); the messages themselves are unchanged.

This file records, as SP12 asks, which of these seams a Goal 9B dispatcher may call directly and
which must stay private.

## What moved

| Unit | Kind | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `SwitchConfigOutcome` | top-level `internal sealed interface` (was a private nested type) | `Configured`, `Unconfigurable` (#742, permanent), `Locked(switch, holder, held, required)` (#1065/#1076, transient) |
| `extractUniqueSwitches(pathInfo)` | read-only | the candidate's switches, each once, in path order |
| `releaseOrphanSwitchLocks(switches)` | mutating, Step 2e.5 | unlocks a lock no train owns (#1065) |
| `reclaimStaleForeignSwitchOwnership(trainId, switches)` | mutating, Step 2e.5 | reclaims a foreign claim whose owner holds no adjacent block (#1076) |
| `configureSwitchesInPath(trainId, pathInfo)` | mutating, Step 2f | sets and locks every traversed switch; classifies the result |
| `registerSwitches(trainId, switches)` | mutating, Step 2f | registers the candidate's switches, skipping foreign-owned ones (and, since #1173, born-stale ones) |
| `releaseCandidateSwitches(trainId, switches)` | mutating, rollback | unregisters own claims, unlocks ownerless locks, leaves foreign ones alone |
| `nearestTrackBefore/After`, `setUpTraversedSwitch`, `switchOwnershipPreCheck`, `MinimalTrackOccupant` | private helpers | unchanged |
| `configureSwitchAt` (2026-10-24 round) | private helper | the extracted per-switch loop body of `configureSwitchesInPath`; `null` = skip |

Kept in the service, on purpose:

- `configureAndRegisterSwitches` — it has to call the scoped rollback, which also rolls back
  blocks; it is now a ten-line orchestration over the new unit.
- `rollbackUnconfigurableCandidate` — block rollback plus switch release; it belongs to the
  release/rollback leaf (#1168), and tests call it on the service.
- `recordSwitchConfigFailureIfGeometric` — the candidate loop's geometric-failure accounting
  (`GeometricFailures`), not switch configuration.

## Seams for a Goal 9B dispatcher

A dispatcher never reaches `SwitchConfigurator` directly; it is `internal` and has no Koin
binding. A dispatcher acts through `PathReservationService` / `InterlockingFacade` only. Within
that rule:

### May be surfaced (read-only)

- **`SwitchConfigOutcome.Locked`** — the one switch fact a dispatcher needs and cannot get today.
  It names the switch, the holding train, the held and the required position. This is exactly the
  "a switch is locked against us" reason that SP5 demand **D3** asks for, and it is transient
  ("wait for `holder`"), unlike `Unconfigurable` ("never try again"). Today the service folds
  `Locked` into `AllPathsBlocked` and drops the attribution. **Goal 9B demand:** carry
  `switch`, `holder`, `held`, `required` through the reservation result or the observation, so a
  dispatcher can watch the holder instead of retrying blind.
- **`extractUniqueSwitches`** — the switches a candidate would set. Read-only and free of side
  effects, so it is safe behind a query; a dispatcher could use it to see which candidates share
  a switch (conflict prediction). Not needed by any dispatcher today.

### Must stay private

- **`configureSwitchesInPath`, `registerSwitches`** — they are correct only inside Step 2f,
  after the blocks are reserved and after Step 2e.5. Called alone, `configureSwitchesInPath`
  locks switches with no block held (an orphan lock), and until #1173 `registerSwitches` made the
  born-stale claim described below.
- **`releaseOrphanSwitchLocks`, `reclaimStaleForeignSwitchOwnership`** — they unlock and
  reclaim other trains' state. Their safety rests on the registry's stale predicate and on running
  just before a candidate is configured; a dispatcher calling them would be a back door around
  interlocking (SI-5).
- **`releaseCandidateSwitches`** — correct only for switches a rolled-back candidate itself
  locked or registered (the caller removes the train's prior switches first). A dispatcher has
  `cancel_route` / `releasePath` for this.

## `registerSwitches` — the born-stale rule (pinned by #1170, fixed by #1173)

`SwitchConfiguratorTest` › `RegisterSwitches` (nested class) pins the behaviour:

- a listed switch is registered and locked only when the train holds a block whose ends include
  it (`PathReservationRegistry.wouldBeStaleClaim` is `false`);
- a listed switch the train holds no such block for is skipped with a debug log line: the claim
  would be stale the moment it is made;
- a foreign-owned switch is skipped silently (no exception).

This is consistent with the #1103 item 3 ruling ("every switch in a reserved path is rolled over
by the train and must stay held") by construction. At Step 2f every candidate block is
registry-held by the train: the new blocks since Step 2d, the owned-prefix blocks since their own
reservation. So every traversed switch has a held adjacent block and stays registered. Only a
switch at a path end, or one in a hand-built list with no held block next to it, is skipped.

**History.** Until #1173 the pin recorded the opposite outcome. For a candidate that lists a switch
it does not traverse (the `zA → vA` PathInfo that ends at vA), `configureSwitchesInPath` skipped vA
and left it unlocked, yet `registerSwitches` claimed and locked it with no block held:
`registry.isStaleSwitchOwnership(vA)` was `true` right after registration, and the born-stale
claim lived until another train's candidate reclaimed it in Step 2e.5. #1173 flipped those two
pins and added a positive case (a held block ending at vA, so vA is registered). The Step 2e.5
`reclaimStaleForeignSwitchOwnership` pre-step stays, because the public
`PathReservationRegistry.registerSwitches` still accepts a claim with no block held.

A mutation check on the original pins (2026-10-24) confirmed they were live: filtering
unconfigured (unlocked) switches out of `registerSwitches` made all four original `RegisterSwitches`
group pins fail (the group has five tests since #1173).

### 2026-10-24 review round (PR #1196)

The owner's threads asked for the long `configureSwitchesInPath` to shrink and its `forEachIndexed`
loop body to be extracted; the round split the loop body verbatim into the private `configureSwitchAt`
(`null` = the path does not genuinely traverse the switch; the loop keeps first-hit-wins `Locked`,
the immediate `Unconfigurable` bail, and the count). The round also added the mutation-proven pins
the Sonar New-Code coverage gate wanted (`new_coverage` 79.6 < 80 on the review head):

- `SwitchConfiguratorTest`: `a plain environment fails the configuration fast` (the
  `SimulationContext` cast), `a switch listed twice is extracted once` (the dedup branch),
  `a failing registry release is contained and the rest of the candidate still releases` (the
  `releaseCandidateSwitches` catch, on a spy of the real registry).
- `PathReservationMergeAbortRollbackTest` (new): the spy-forced Step 2i merge abort releases the
  candidate transactionally — blocks, switches, START signal — leaves nothing stored, and the
  attempt reports `AllPathsBlocked`.
- Each new pin was proven by mutating its guarded line and watching only that pin fail; the
  mutations were reverted afterwards.

Non-pinnable coverage leftovers in `SwitchConfigurator.kt` (each with its reason):

- the file-scope `logger` initializer — the same SP12 leftover every moved class carries;
- `MinimalTrackOccupant`'s three `TrackOccupant` members — `DynamicRailSwitch.setUpPath` accepts
  the occupant but never reads it, and the interface declares no default implementations;
- the defence-in-depth `SwitchLockedException` catch in `setUpTraversedSwitch` (and the `Locked`
  bookkeeping that follows it) — unreachable single-threaded: the ownership pre-check and
  `setUpPath` read the same state, so only a concurrent mutation between the two reads could
  diverge them;
- the "segments undeterminable" lenient skip and the separator-neighbour branches of
  `nearestTrackBefore`/`nearestTrackAfter` — on real fixture networks every separator neighbour
  pair is separated by a track block, and a block not at the separator's grid position makes
  `getSegment` throw `SimulationException` instead of returning `null`, so these shapes cannot be
  driven on a real object.
